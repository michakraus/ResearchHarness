The builder-critic loop ​

A part of a plan is one pull request with a spec. The builder-critic loop takes a part from its spec to an open pull request with no question to you on the way, unless the spec leaves a decision that only you can take. One agent builds, a second agent that has not seen the builder's reasoning judges, and the loop repeats until a critic passes the part on a green suite. This page describes the two loops of the harness, build-part and build-reviewed, and the choice between them.

Why a blind critic ​

A context that wrote the code knows why each line is right, so it reads what it meant, not what it wrote. A new context with only the spec and the branch reads the code as a user of it would. The critic therefore never sees the builder's report, and each round has a new critic. A critic that judged one round would carry its own reasons into the next.

The same holds for the builder: after each critic round, the loop starts a new builder on the same worktree, with the last builder's report as a handoff note. A resumed builder carries its whole context, and a new one starts from the reports, which costs less and leaves fewer defects.

The tier of a part ​

The plan gives each part a tier. The tier chooses how much review the part gets:

tierhow it is builtfor
decisionnot built; it waits for your decisiona part whose spec holds a question that only you can answer
buildthe full loop of build-part: two critics in round 1, verify rounds, the advisor and the arbitratora part that draws a security boundary, predicts what another tool reads, or has a slow gate
reviewedthe small loop of build-reviewed: one builder, one critic, one fix round, and your session's reada small part with a fast gate, whose every edge the spec decides
directyour session or a workflow makes the change, with no critica mechanical change whose every check is a command, such as a rename across many repositories

The full loop: build-part ​

From a task to a pull request with build-part. The plan-parts skill writes the parts table, the list of the parts of a plan, and build-part runs one part. In the first critic round, two critics judge the work at high effort beside a run of the full suite. After a FAIL, a new builder fixes the work, and one critic judges the fix. The second FAIL goes to the arbitrator. After a PASS, a new builder finishes the part and opens the pull request. The figure shows the default models of Claude Code. Your models.toml can add a council of critics on other models for OpenCode and oh-my-pi.

  1. The spec gate. A build part starts only when its spec holds the sections Decided at the edges and Tests catch. The script spec-gate.py checks this. Where a section is missing, the advisor writes it, or the part waits for you.
  2. The build. The skill picks the builder and the critic from the part's language: julia-builder and julia-critic for Julia, python-builder and python-critic for Python, and part-builder and part-critic elsewhere. The builder works in its own worktree. It writes the tests first, then the code, then runs the mutants of the spec and its own. It returns ready for critic with a table of its mutants and a table of the text lines it checked. A report with an open survivor is not ready.
  3. Round 1. Two critics judge the whole part at high effort, each blind to the other and to the builder. Beside them, julia-test-runner runs the full suite. The round fails when either critic fails, and its findings are the union of both reports. One draw of a critic misses defects that a second one finds, and round 1 is the one broad search of the loop.
  4. A fix and a verify round. After a FAIL, a new builder fixes each blocking defect and the other defects that its branch causes, and records the rest in the repository's KNOWN_ISSUES.md. One new critic then judges only the fix: it gets the earlier blocking defects and their reproducers, never the builder's report.
  5. The arbitrator. The second FAIL goes to the arbitrator, not to a third round. Two fix attempts that did not close mean that the approach must change. The arbitrator reads the whole loop and chooses one move: continue, redesign, decide, a test round, a fresh build, a rerun, a known issue, or park the part for you. A FAIL of the round after its ruling stops the loop, and the skill reports to you.
  6. The finish. After a PASS on a green suite, a new builder rereads the text of the whole branch, runs julia-branch-verifier and opens the pull request. The code does not change after the PASS, except for the verifier's fixes and fixes of text.
  7. After the loop. The skill records the rounds, the decisions and the rulings in the part's section of the task file. Each defect that a critic found late, in a class that the edge catalogue does not hold, becomes a new line of that catalogue.

What a critic reports ​

A critic returns Verdict: PASS or Verdict: FAIL, an evidence table with one row per clause of the spec, its blocking defects and its other defects. A blocking defect is a failure inside the domain of a clause: the part does not do what the spec says. Each one names its reproducer and one cause:

causethe defect
wrong resulta wrong output, or an unmet clause on an input
evidencethe code is right on every input tried, but a test cannot fail or a mutant survives
speca clause or an edge is undecided, or two rules disagree on what the branch must do
fix regressionin a verify round: the defect is in lines that the fix added or changed
neighbourin a verify round: a new input of a class that an earlier defect names, in code that predicts what another tool reads or does
harnessa tool, the environment, a prompt or a moved main, not the branch

An other defect is real, but outside every clause; the builder fixes it when its branch causes it, and records it otherwise. The causes decide the next move. A round whose every blocking defect is harness does not count. A spec defect on a rule outside the part goes to the arbitrator, because the same rule reaches the other parts of the plan. When the arbitrator rules, the cause that dominates picks its move: neighbour or fix regression asks for a redesign, evidence for a round that writes only tests.

Questions inside the loop ​

A builder that meets a question that the spec does not decide stops and names the question. The skill gives it to the advisor, which decides it and says how sure it is and whether the decision can change inside the pull request. A decision that cannot change, such as a public interface, parks the part for you. Every decision goes into the task file before the next round, so that the critic judges against the same text as the builder.

The small loop: build-reviewed ​

From a task to a pull request with build-reviewed. The skill spawns one part-builder, and one part-critic judges its work. After a FAIL, a new builder fixes the findings, and your session checks the fix before it tells the builder to finish the part. With a PASS, your session reads the result, and then it tells the builder to finish the part.

A part of tier reviewed is small, and its spec decides every edge. build-reviewed runs it in your session on the large model, with one builder and one blind critic. A FAIL gets one fix round, and your session checks the fix itself; a FAIL that the fix round does not close goes to you. Your session decides the questions inside the part, so no advisor and no arbitrator run. Before the finish, your session reads the result: it is the second reviewer.

One worktree for each part ​

Each part is built in a worktree: a second checkout of the repository on its own branch, below ~/Research/.worktrees/. The main checkout stays on main, and two parts can be built at the same time in two worktrees. Each new builder and critic of the same part works on the same worktree. The WorktreeCreate hook makes each worktree from origin/HEAD, at the place that the research tree names. Git safety says how a worktree is made and removed.

Several parts at once ​

Parts that touch no common file can run at the same time, each in its own worktree. The collision map of the plan says which parts share files; Tasks describes it. A part that is blocked by another starts only after that part merges, in a worktree from the new main. Parts that only add lines to a shared CHANGELOG.md resolve it by a rebase.
